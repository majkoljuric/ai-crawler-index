Read like a crawler

The rule that actually applies

When a site has both a general rule and one written for a specific crawler, the specific one wins — exactly as the crawler itself would read it.

Deliberate vs default

Only one of them is a decision

Most sites have blanket rules that catch every crawler by accident. Naming an AI crawler is a choice someone made about it. We show you which is which — filled dots were named, hollow ones just got swept up.

No guessing

A gap is shown as a gap

If a site doesn't respond, it's marked not yet checked — never quietly counted as welcoming every crawler.

What this is

A request, not a lock

These rules record what a site asked crawlers to do. They aren't enforcement, and not every crawler obeys them.

What the statuses mean. Blocked means the whole site is closed to that crawler. Partial means some paths are closed and the rest are open. Allowed means nothing is closed to it. Not yet checked means the site didn't respond when we last looked — a gap in our coverage, never a claim that the crawler is welcome. Which sites are in the index.
